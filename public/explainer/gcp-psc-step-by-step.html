GCP Private Service Connect, Step by Step
← ExplainersBhupendra YadavNov 2026bhupixb.github.io
The problem
A SaaS provider runs a database in its own GCP project. A customer wants to reach it privately from their own VPC: no VPC peering, no public IPs, no merging of IP ranges. The two VPCs may even use the same range. Private Service Connect (PSC) is GCP’s answer: the provider (the producer) publishes a service, and the customer (the consumer) gets a private IP in their own VPC that leads to it.
What this page is
Setting up PSC takes five resources. On the producer side: a PSC NAT subnet, a service attachment and a firewall rule. On the consumer side: an endpoint and a private DNS record. I could follow the steps, but I could not see why each piece was needed, so I asked Claude to build this page while I was learning how PSC works for YugabyteDB Managed on GCP.
It is an interactive walkthrough. Build the connection one resource at a time, send a packet to see how far it gets, and remove a piece to see exactly what breaks without it.
Words used below
YBM, “we”, “our VPC” = YugabyteDB Managed, the producer. Customer = the consumer. All names and IPs are made-up examples.
Assumes
Basic GCP networking: VPCs, subnets, firewall rules, internal load balancers.

Build a PSC connection, one piece at a time

Each step adds one piece. Press Send a packet to see how far a packet gets. Press the red button to remove the piece and see what breaks. When you know why each piece exists, the whole design follows.

The one idea to hold

The customer VPC and our VPC have no routes to each other, and here they even use the same range (10.1.0.0/16). The client and one of our nodes both have IP 10.1.0.11. Every piece below exists to make one connection work between two networks that cannot see each other.
